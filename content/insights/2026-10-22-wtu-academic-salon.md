  Invited Talk at Wuhan Textile University’s 171st Academic Salon
---

On 22 October 2026, at the invitation of **Professor Yunbin He**, I gave a talk at **Wuhan Textile University’s 171st Academic Salon**, organized by the School of Microelectronics. The salon focused on optoelectronic thin-film materials and surface characterization. I shared the programme with **Professor Janez Kovač** from the Jožef Stefan Institute, Slovenia, who also holds a professorship at the Jožef Stefan International Postgraduate School.

My presentation, *“Strain Engineering and Buffer Layer Design in VO₂ Thin Film Structures: From Phase Transition Mechanisms to Smart Window Applications,”* connected the physics of strain-controlled phase transitions with the design of thermochromic coatings. I discussed how strain and strain gradients influence switching in VO₂, and how buffer-layer selection affects film growth and optical performance for smart-window applications.

Professor Kovač’s presentation covered X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) for analyzing surfaces, thin films and multilayer structures. Together, the two talks brought complementary perspectives on thin-film design and the characterization of surfaces and interfaces.

## Research ties and international collaboration

During my visit to Wuhan Textile University, I also joined Professor Kovač and Cazibe Badenstedt, a postgraduate student at Justus Liebig University Giessen, for discussions with the university’s Office of International Affairs and School of Microelectronics. Drawing on my academic and research experience, I shared the background to my research ties with Wuhan Textile University.

The meeting identified preliminary directions for cooperation, including joint research proposals involving Chinese and European partners, joint postgraduate training and short-term exchanges. Discussions also explored a proposed international cooperation platform focused on new semiconductor materials and advanced optoelectronic devices. These conversations added an institutional perspective to the scientific exchange, connecting thin-film research with opportunities for future collaboration.
